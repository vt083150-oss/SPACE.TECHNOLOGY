🌌 What Is Space?


Space is the vast region beyond Earth's atmosphere. It contains planets, moons, stars, galaxies, asteroids, comets, nebulae, and many other celestial objects. Space is not completely empty—it contains matter, radiation, magnetic fields, and other forms of energy.

Mercury


☿ Mercury — The Closest Planet to the Sun Mercury is the smallest planet in our Solar System and the planet closest to the Sun. Despite its small size, Mercury has a fascinating surface covered with craters and experiences extreme temperature changes.

🌌 Quick Facts

Position from Sun: 1st planet

Type: Terrestrial (rocky) planet

Diameter: About 4,879 km

Distance from Sun: About 57.9 million km

Length of a year: 88 Earth days

Length of a day: About 59 Earth days

Moons: 0

Rings: None

Atmosphere: Very thin exosphere

Temperature: About 430°C during the day and can fall to around −180°C at night.

🪨 Surface

Mercury's surface is rocky and heavily covered with impact craters, making it look somewhat like Earth's Moon. One of its most notable features is the Caloris Basin, a huge impact crater created by an ancient collision.

☀️ Extreme Temperatures

Mercury is closest to the Sun, but it is not the hottest planet—Venus is hotter because of its thick atmosphere. Mercury has almost no atmosphere to retain heat, so temperatures change dramatically between day and night.

🔄 Orbit and Rotation

Mercury travels around the Sun very quickly, completing one orbit in only 88 Earth days. It rotates slowly, taking about 59 Earth days to rotate once on its axis. Interestingly, one solar day on Mercury—from one sunrise to the next—lasts about 176 Earth days.

🧲 Magnetic Field

Mercury has a global magnetic field, although it is much weaker than Earth's. Scientists believe this magnetic field is related to its large metallic core.

🚀 Space Missions

Mercury has been studied by several spacecraft, including Mariner 10, MESSENGER, and BepiColombo. These missions have helped scientists understand Mercury's surface, interior, magnetic field, and geological history.

1. Introduction to Earth


Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet that supports life. It contains liquid water, a protective atmosphere, and suitable temperatures that allow millions of species to survive.


Earth is often called the Blue Planet because approximately 71% of its surface is covered by water.


- Planet type: Terrestrial planet


Position: Third planet from the Sun


- Age: Approximately 4.54 billion years


- Shape: Oblate spheroid (slightly flattened at the poles)


- Natural satellite: The Moon


- Main life-supporting features: Liquid water, atmosphere, and suitable temperatures
